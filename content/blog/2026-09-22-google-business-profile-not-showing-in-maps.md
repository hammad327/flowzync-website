Your profile is verified, the details are right, and you still cannot find yourself in Maps. Before changing anything, there are three questions worth answering in order, because the fix for each is completely different.

## First: are you actually not showing, or are you not showing *there*?

Local results are ranked heavily by proximity. Google decides what to show partly from where the searcher is standing, which means there is no single answer to "do I rank" — only "do I rank from here".

A plumber in north Leeds may be in the top three for someone in Chapel Allerton and invisible to someone in Morley, eight miles away. Both are "Leeds". Neither result is broken.

So test properly. Your own screen is the least reliable source available: you are signed into the owning account and you are usually at the business address, and both inflate what you see. Open an incognito window at minimum. Better, use a grid tool that samples the same search from points across your area — the picture is usually "strong within two miles, gone by five", which is normal and tells you where the real gap is.

If you rank near the business and not further out, nothing is broken. You are looking at the proximity ceiling, and the only lever is authority: reviews, relevance, and a website that genuinely covers the places you serve.

## Second: is the profile actually live?

**Suspended.** It happens more quietly than people expect. A suspended profile disappears from search while still looking normal in some views. Check the account for a notice and whether the listing opens from a logged-out browser.

**Unverified or verification lapsed.** Re-verification is sometimes requested after an edit, particularly an address or category change, and the profile can drop out while it is pending.

**Duplicate listings.** Two profiles for the same business split your signals and Google may be showing the one you are not looking at — often an old auto-generated listing with the wrong phone number. Search your business name and address, and look for anything you do not control.

**Marked permanently closed.** Occasionally by a competitor, occasionally by a stray user suggestion. Worth checking once.

## Third: is the category right?

This is the most common fixable cause and the most underrated.

Your **primary** category does most of the work in deciding which searches you are eligible for. An emergency plumber whose primary category is "Contractor" is competing in the wrong set. A clinic listed as "Medical Centre" rather than "Dental Clinic" will not surface for dental searches regardless of how good the profile is.

Pick the primary category that matches the single search you most want to win. Add secondary categories for genuine services, but do not pile them on — relevance gets diluted, and categories you cannot evidence invite problems.

Look at who currently occupies the top three for your target search and check their primary category. It is public, and it tells you what Google considers the right answer for that query.

## The things that move the needle after that

**Reviews, and recency.** Volume matters, but a steady flow matters more than a cluster from two years ago. Reviews that mention the service and the place — "fitted a new boiler in Headingley" — do more than "great service".

**A website that matches.** The profile and the site should agree on name, address and phone, exactly. Beyond that, the site needs real pages for the services and areas the profile claims. A profile asserting you serve eight towns with a website that mentions none of them is an unsupported claim.

**Services and products filled in.** Most profiles leave these empty. They are additional surface for matching a query and they take an afternoon.

**Photos, added regularly.** Not a ranking lever people can prove, but profiles that look active and show real premises and real work get chosen more often from the results that do appear.

**Posts.** Low effort, modest effect, but they keep the profile from looking abandoned.

## What will not help

Keyword-stuffing the business name. "Smith Plumbing — Emergency Plumber Leeds 24/7" is against the guidelines, it is reportable by any competitor, and it is one of the faster routes to a suspension.

Fake addresses, virtual offices and relatives' spare rooms in neighbouring towns. This is the oldest trick in local SEO and the most actively policed.

Buying reviews. Detectable, removable, and it puts the whole profile at risk.

## If you are a service area business

Hide the address if you do not receive customers there — that part is correct. But understand what you have bought: Google still ranks you from your registered address. Adding twenty service areas describes where you will travel; it does not place you in twenty towns.

The way to earn presence in a town you do not sit in is a genuine page about working there, real reviews from customers there, and time. There is no setting for it.
