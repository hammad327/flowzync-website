A visitor sends a project enquiry and lands on a page that says “Thank you.” Did the form arrive? When will someone reply? A confirmation page can answer both questions while the visitor is still paying attention.

## Confirm receipt and set expectations

Say exactly what was submitted and what happens next. For example, “We received your quote request. Our team reviews enquiries during business hours and replies by email.” Add a response window only if your team can meet it. If you offer an alternative for urgent jobs, include the relevant phone number and hours.

## Give one next action

A design studio might offer the project brief so a client can gather examples before the call. A contractor might explain how to take useful photos. Choose something that moves the same enquiry forward. Keep sensitive form answers out of the URL and confirmation page.

Test the success message on mobile, then submit invalid details to confirm it cannot appear when the form fails. If you use the page for measurement, follow our [GA4 form tracking guide](/blog/ga4-generate-lead-form-tracking) so a refresh or direct visit does not create a second lead.
