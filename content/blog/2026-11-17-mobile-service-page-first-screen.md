A homeowner lands on a plumbing page and sees a full-screen photo with the headline “Quality You Can Trust.” They still need to know whether the company fixes the particular problem and covers their address. That is what the first screen should settle.

## Read the page like a new visitor

Open one service page on a phone. In the first view, look for the service, who it is for, and a useful next action. If your headline could appear on a competitor in a different industry, write it again with the job in mind. “Boiler repairs in Norwich” tells a reader more than “Comfort starts here.” The supporting line can explain availability or common problems the team handles.

## Give the button a clear job

Choose a label that says what happens. “Request a repair quote” sets a different expectation from “Book a visit.” If an urgent call and a planned quote both make sense, make the distinction obvious. Put evidence immediately below the introduction so readers who need reassurance can continue without hunting.

Ask someone to tell you what the page offers after looking for five seconds. Then ask them to show how they would contact you. Record where they hesitate, and change the first thing that caused the hesitation. See our [homepage audit](/blog/homepage-audit-service-business) for a broader review.
