A page racks up impressions and barely gets clicked. You change its title. A week later clicks fall again. Before rewriting another version, look at which searches contributed the impressions.

## Narrow the report to one decision

In the [Search Console Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553), filter to one page and compare two equivalent periods. Open the Queries tab. Look at the queries with meaningful impressions, then separate branded and non-branded terms. Compare device and country where relevant. An average across more queries or devices can move while your main buyer query stays unchanged.

Consider two searches for a WordPress service page. Someone looking for “WordPress design agency” may be ready to shortlist a provider. Someone looking for “WordPress design ideas” may just want examples. If the second query suddenly contributes most of the impressions, lower overall CTR tells you little about how the page performs for the first one.

## Choose the fix that fits the evidence

If position dropped on your intended query, inspect what now ranks above you and what those pages answer. If position stayed comparable but the snippet is vague, rewrite the title and on-page heading to clarify the offer. Check the page for an accurate description and useful evidence. If the wrong page shows up, improve internal links and distinguish the pages' purposes.

Keep a dated note of the edit. Compare the same query group later, allowing time for recrawling. Treat small samples as directional and check whether qualified enquiries changed too.
