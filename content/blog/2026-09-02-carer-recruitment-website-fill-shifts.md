Most care providers describe recruitment as their hardest problem, and most assume it is about pay. Pay matters. But a large share of carer applications are lost to three things that have nothing to do with money and can be fixed in about a week.

## One: the advert does not say what the job pays

A carer scrolling a jobs board at eleven at night is applying to four or five providers in one sitting. They filter on pay and shift pattern, in that order.

An advert that says "competitive rates" is not read as discreet. It is read as low, or as a conversation the candidate will have to have before they know whether the job is worth their time — so they skip it and apply to the one that stated £13.50 an hour plus mileage.

Publishing the rate also saves you interviewing people who were never going to accept it, which is time you do not have.

## Two: nobody acknowledges the application

This is the big one, and it is identical to the problem on the family enquiry side.

Someone applies at eleven at night. They have applied to four providers. Nothing happens until Tuesday, when your office manager gets to the inbox between two other urgent things. By then, one of the other four has already acknowledged, arranged a call and booked an interview.

You did not lose that carer on pay. You lost them on silence.

An automatic acknowledgement within seconds — naming who will be in touch and when, and what the next step is — costs nothing to run and changes the outcome more than another fifty pence an hour would.

## Three: the paperwork stalls and nobody chases

DBS, right to work, references, training certificates. Every one of these is a point where an application quietly stops.

The candidate meant to send it. They forgot. Nobody chased. Three weeks later they are working for someone else, and your vacancy is still open — while you are turning down packages because you are short-staffed.

Automatic document chasing is unglamorous and it is probably the highest-return automation available to a care provider. A reminder at day two, day five and day nine, sent without anyone remembering to send it, recovers a meaningful share of applications that would otherwise evaporate.

## What a recruitment page should actually contain

- **The hourly rate**, or a genuine range
- **Shift patterns** — including whether there are guaranteed hours, and whether weekends are required
- **Mileage or travel time**, stated plainly, because in home care this materially changes what the job pays
- **What training you provide** and whether it is paid
- **Progression** — what a carer can move into, because the good ones are thinking about it
- **What the work actually involves** on a normal day, not a list of values
- **An application that takes under three minutes on a phone**, because that is where it will be filled in

That last point is worth being strict about. A ten-field form with a CV upload requirement, on a mobile, at eleven at night, loses most of the people who started it. Take a name, a number and availability. Ask the rest once you are talking.

## Why this belongs on your own website

Job boards work, but they rent you the candidate. Everyone on that board sees the same advert next to four competitors, and you pay again next month.

A recruitment section on your own site accumulates search presence for "care jobs in [your town]", which is a genuinely winnable local search that most providers ignore entirely. It also lets you say more than a board template allows, and it costs nothing per application.

Use both. Just stop treating your own site as the place where a PDF job description goes to be ignored.

## The uncomfortable part

None of this fixes a provider where the pay is genuinely below the local market, the rota is chaotic, or carers leave within three months. Automation will get you more applications and more interviews; it will not keep people who arrive and find the job is not what was described.

But if you are losing candidates in the gap between them applying and you replying — and most providers are — that is a week of work and it is free thereafter.
