If you run a business that serves a place, **the highest-return hour you will ever spend on marketing is completing your Google Business Profile properly**. Not partially. Every field, real photographs, correct categories, actual hours.

Most local businesses do about 40% of it, then pay an agency to do things that matter considerably less. So here is the list, in the order I would actually do it — and almost all of it is free.

## The foundations

**1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile.** Every field. The primary category is the most important single choice on the whole profile, so get it right rather than approximately right — "emergency plumber" and "plumber" behave differently. Add secondary categories for everything else you do. Add services with descriptions. Add real photographs, not stock. Set exact hours including holidays.

**2. Get your name, address and phone number identical everywhere.** Your website, Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook, Yell, industry directories. Keep the underlying details accurate and recognisable. An abbreviation such as Street versus St does not by itself establish a ranking problem; wrong addresses, duplicate profiles and outdated phone numbers deserve priority.

**3. Make real contact information easy to find as text.** Publish a customer-facing address only when appropriate. A remote business should explain how it works rather than inventing premises.

**4. Add LocalBusiness structured data.** It tells search engines your details in a format they read unambiguously. If you do not have a public address — you are mobile, or you work from home — use a service-area business setup rather than inventing an address. Fake addresses get profiles suspended, and reinstatement is genuinely painful.

## Ratings and reviews

**5. Invite customers to leave an honest Google review.** This is the second biggest lever after the profile itself, and almost nobody does it systematically. Send a direct link, ask within a day or two of finishing the job, and make it one tap.

**6. Reply to every review.** All of them, good and bad. Replies help customers understand how you handle feedback. Do not treat replying as a guaranteed ranking factor.

**7. Never buy reviews.** Beyond being against Google's rules, in the US, UK, EU and Australia it is a consumer protection offence with real penalties. The detection is much better than people assume.

## The website itself

**8. One page per service, with the place name used naturally.** "Emergency plumber in Wakefield" as a heading, in the text, in the page title. Naturally — three times in a paragraph reads as spam to both readers and search engines.

**9. Separate pages for separate towns, but only where you can write something real.** If you cannot say something genuinely specific about a town, do not make a page for it. Thin duplicated location pages are demoted as a group.

**10. Make it fast on a phone.** Local searches are overwhelmingly mobile, often on poor signal, frequently from someone standing outside deciding whether to call. [Speed matters here more than it does for most sites](/blog/does-website-speed-affect-seo-rankings).

**11. Make the phone number tappable,** and put a call button where a thumb naturally rests. This sounds trivial and it measurably is not.

**12. Make location information useful.** A map can help visitors to a real customer-facing premises; it is not necessary for a remote-only business and must not imply a staffed office that does not exist.

## Ongoing, and genuinely optional

**13. Post to your Google Business Profile occasionally.** Offers, updates, completed jobs. Monthly is plenty. The effect is modest but the cost is near zero.

**14. Get listed in local directories that matter in your trade.** Chamber of commerce, trade bodies, local news, sponsorship pages. Quality over quantity — twenty relevant local mentions beat two hundred generic directory listings, and the generic ones can actively hurt.

## What I would not pay for yet

**Bulk directory submissions.** Hundreds of low-quality listings do almost nothing and occasionally cause harm.

**"Guaranteed number one rankings".** Nobody controls Google's results. Anyone guaranteeing a position is either lying or planning to rank you for something nobody searches.

**Monthly SEO retainers before the free work is done.** If your Google Business Profile is half-complete and you have eleven reviews, an agency retainer is being spent on the difficult 20% while the easy 80% sits untouched. Do this list first. Then, if you still need help, at least you will be paying for the hard part.

## What to actually expect

For a business in a smaller town with modest competition, doing this list properly, you should see movement in local pack results within four to eight weeks and a meaningful change in enquiries within three to six months. In a competitive city, longer — and "web design London" style head terms may never be realistic, which is fine, because service-plus-neighbourhood terms convert better anyway.

The unglamorous truth about local SEO is that most of it is admin. It is a Tuesday afternoon of filling in forms carefully, and then asking for reviews forever. That is genuinely most of it.
