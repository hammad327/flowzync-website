A contractor covers five towns. One gets most of the emergency work, two share the same team and the last two are occasional visits. Publishing five pages with identical copy gives a reader little reason to open any particular one.

## Start with the decision a visitor needs to make

List where you actually work and which services are available there. If coverage rules are the same throughout the region, a good service-area page can answer the question clearly. Include postcodes where they help, minimum job size if relevant, and how to check availability.

A separate town page earns its place when it can explain something distinct. A gutter company might show an approved example of work on a specific property type, explain access constraints and tell the reader how scheduling works in that area. Google’s [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe the problem with regional doorway pages that funnel readers onward without adding value.

## Connect the location to the actual service

Give the town page a direct route to the relevant service and enquiry form. Link to it from your coverage index with the place name. After it has enough data, compare the search queries and enquiries for that page with the regional guide. If users keep asking the same coverage question, fix that answer before publishing another town.
