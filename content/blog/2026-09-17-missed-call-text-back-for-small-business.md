Someone rings your business. Nobody picks up — you are on a ladder, mid-treatment, with another client. They hang up and ring the next number on the search results page.

Missed call text back puts an SMS in their hand within seconds instead: *we missed you, here is when we will ring back, or tell us what you need.* It costs very little and it is one of the few automations that can pay for itself on one recovered job.

It is also one of the easiest to set up badly.

## What is actually happening

Your business number routes through a system that watches call status. When a call ends unanswered — rung out, rejected, or hit voicemail — it triggers an SMS to that number. The reply comes back into a shared inbox where someone can pick it up.

Three parts, and the third is the one people skip. An automation that fires texts nobody reads is worse than no automation, because you have now promised a response and not delivered one.

## Who it helps

**Trades.** Boiler failures, leaks, lockouts, electrical faults. The customer is ringing in order, and whoever responds first usually wins. You cannot answer from under a sink; a text sent while you are under there keeps you in the running.

**Clinics and practices.** Dental, physio, veterinary, private GP. Reception is on another line or with a patient. The caller is often booking rather than browsing and will go elsewhere rather than wait.

**Salons, barbers, beauty.** Mid-appointment, hands full, phone across the room. The appointment economy runs on who answers.

**Care providers.** A family ringing several agencies in one evening. Speed here is worth more than almost anything else on the website, because the enquiry is urgent and emotional and the first responsive provider earns disproportionate trust.

## Who it does not help

If your enquiries arrive by email and your customers expect a two-day turnaround, this solves nothing. If you already answer nearly every call, you are adding a system to manage a problem you do not have. And if the reason you miss calls is that you are at capacity and turning work away, a faster response just produces more conversations ending in "sorry, we are booked until March".

Worth being honest about that before buying anything.

## The setup mistakes

**Nobody watches the inbox.** The single biggest one. The text invites a reply, the reply lands in a system nobody has open, and the customer is now more annoyed than if you had never texted. If the replies are not going somewhere a human looks within minutes during working hours, do not switch it on.

**The text sounds automated.** "Thank you for contacting us. Your enquiry is important to us." Everyone knows what that is. Write it as the person who would have answered the phone: *Hi, Dave here from Medway Drains — sorry I missed you, I'm on a job until about 3. Text me what's happening and I'll call straight after.*

**Promising a callback time you will not meet.** "We'll call you right back" and then three hours of silence is worse than the original missed call. Say something you can keep, including on your busiest day.

**Firing outside working hours with no adjustment.** A 10pm missed call should get a different message from a 10am one. "We're closed now, first thing tomorrow" is fine. "We'll ring you back shortly" at eleven at night is not.

**Texting landlines.** They do not receive SMS. Most systems detect it; some do not, and you end up believing you responded when nothing arrived. Check what yours does.

**No opt-out.** A transactional reply to an inbound call is on reasonable footing, but the moment you follow up with anything promotional you are in marketing-consent territory. Keep the text transactional and keep an opt-out route.

## What a good one looks like

> Hi — Sarah at Bluebell Care. Sorry we missed your call, we're with a client until 2pm. Reply here with what you need and we'll ring you straight after, or call back any time after 2. — 01634 xxxxxx

Names a person. Explains the silence. Gives a time that is real. Offers two routes. Nothing is being sold.

## Where it sits in the bigger picture

Missed call text back is a patch on a leak, and a good one. It is not a lead generation system. If the problem is that the phone does not ring enough, this changes nothing — that is a visibility problem and belongs with your website, your Google Business Profile and local search.

Fix the leak when calls are arriving and draining away. Fix visibility when they are not arriving at all. Doing them in the wrong order is the usual mistake.
