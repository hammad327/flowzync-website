A family looking for home care does not search the way a marketing plan assumes. They do not type "domiciliary care provider". They type things like *home care near me*, *live in carer cost uk*, *care for mum after hospital discharge*, or *dementia care [town]* — and then they open whatever answers.

Getting found is mostly about being present on three surfaces, and knowing which fight is not worth having.

## The fight you cannot win

You will not outrank the national directories for generic terms. Homecare.co.uk, Trusted Carers and the rest have tens of thousands of pages, a page for every town in the country, and years of accumulated links. For "home care agency" nationally, that is settled.

This matters because a lot of agencies spend money trying anyway, and because the directories' business model is to rank for your market and sell you back the enquiry. Paying for a listing is a legitimate choice; paying an agency to beat them organically on national terms usually is not.

The winnable fight is local, specific and need-based. That is where families actually search.

## Surface one: the map pack

For anything with local intent, the three map results sit above the organic listings and take a large share of the clicks. This is the highest-value surface available to a care provider and it is free.

**Your primary category decides eligibility.** "Home Health Care Service" and "Nursing Agency" surface for different searches. Pick the one matching the search you most want. Look at who occupies the top three in your town and check theirs — it is public information.

**Reviews do most of the remaining work.** Volume, recency and specificity. A review mentioning dementia care in a named town is worth several saying "lovely people". Asking is the hard part: the best moment is after a review meeting that went well, and the request has to come from the carer or coordinator the family actually knows.

**Proximity sets a ceiling you cannot remove.** You will rank strongly near your registered address and fade with distance. A service area business is still ranked from its address — adding fifteen towns describes where you travel, it does not place you there.

## Surface two: your own pages

**A page per town you genuinely cover.** Not thirty near-identical pages with the name swapped — Google handles that badly and families see through it immediately. A page for a town you actually serve, mentioning the areas within it, the hospital families are usually discharged from, how far your carers travel and roughly how quickly you can start there. If you cannot write something specific about a town, you probably should not have a page for it.

**A page per service.** Live-in care, dementia care, respite, companionship, personal care, end of life. These are distinct searches with distinct intent and they cannot share one page. "Dementia care at home" is a different family in a different situation from "respite care for two weeks".

**The question pages.** What home care costs, what happens at an assessment, the difference between home care and a care home, how quickly care can start after a hospital discharge. These are high-intent searches with very little good local content, because most providers write about themselves rather than answering questions.

That last group is where the easiest wins are. A provider that answers the cost question honestly for their area is often the only one doing it.

## Surface three: AI assistants

A growing share of these questions now get asked of an assistant rather than a search box, and the answer gets assembled from pages that state things plainly.

What gets quoted is not clever writing. It is a clear question as a heading, answered in the first two sentences underneath, in text that stands on its own without the rest of the page. FAQ sections work unusually well for this, which is why they are worth having on every service and town page rather than one buried FAQ page.

Nothing here conflicts with ordinary SEO. The same structure helps both.

## The order that works

**Google Business Profile first.** Categories, services, photos, and a real process for asking for reviews. Fastest to move, costs nothing.

**Then the service pages**, because they convert better than town pages and there are fewer of them.

**Then towns**, honestly and one at a time, starting with the ones you have real clients in.

**Then the question pages**, which compound slowly and are the ones AI assistants reach for.

**Then, maybe, the directories** — as paid lead generation while the rest matures, with the cost per enquiry tracked rather than assumed.

## The thing that undoes all of it

A family who finds you and then waits two days for a reply is a family who has already signed with someone else. Visibility and response are one system. Getting found is only worth the effort if the enquiry is answered the same hour it arrives — and for care, in the evening too, because that is when families sit down and start ringing.
