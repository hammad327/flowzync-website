“Order by Friday” sounds clear until a customer orders at 9 PM and discovers the last parcel collection was at noon. Start with the last time your team can actually hand a packed order to the carrier.

## Work backward from fulfilment

For each market and delivery method, note the carrier's published collection schedule, the expected transit time, and your own processing time. Separate warehouse dispatch from carrier delivery. Add a buffer for busy days, and give made-to-order or pre-order products their own rule. Write the order deadline with a time zone and say whether it assumes the item is in stock.

A hypothetical shop might stop promising standard holiday delivery on Tuesday at noon while keeping a Wednesday express option for eligible postcodes. That can work only if both the checkout method and the warehouse process agree. Check the actual dates with your carrier and team before publishing them.

## Put the promise where decisions happen

Use a short notice on affected product pages and a more specific message beside delivery choices when you know the destination. Review the [WooCommerce shipping zones guide](https://woocommerce.com/document/setting-up-shipping-zones/) if availability differs by area. Test an in-stock order, a mixed basket with a slower item, and an address outside the express zone.

Plan what the site will say just after the deadline. A clear next dispatch date or pickup option helps the customer choose. Give customer support the same table and schedule removal of expired banners. If the checkout displays an unexpected method, run through your test addresses and confirm which shipping zone matches each one.
