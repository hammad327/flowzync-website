Your new site looks great at launch. Then someone changes the homepage headline, adds two lines, and the tablet layout starts clipping the text. A useful Elementor handover gives staff a safe place to practise common edits.

## Train on next week’s changes

Pick three tasks from the team’s real calendar. Replace a service photo, change a staff bio and update the enquiry button. Have the person responsible perform the changes on a staging copy while you watch. Note where they stop or where a setting looks easier to change than the intended one.

Separate everyday content edits from reusable design settings. Show which sections appear on several pages. Explain what a global change will affect, and give staff a way to preview their update on mobile before publishing. Use [Elementor’s help center](https://elementor.com/help/) for version-specific controls.

## Leave a short recovery guide

Record a brief screen capture using the actual site. Point out how to review revisions and who owns backups, updates and troubleshooting. Give the team a written list of accounts they control, such as hosting and the domain. Agree how they should request a larger change when a template needs design work.
