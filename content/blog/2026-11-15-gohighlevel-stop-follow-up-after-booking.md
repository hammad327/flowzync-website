A lead books a call at lunchtime. At 4 PM they receive “Would you like to book a call?” Their booking is fine. The follow-up sequence has missed the change in their status.

## Trace the event that means booked

Start with one test contact. Submit the live-equivalent enquiry form, then book through the calendar you send to prospects. Check how the booking appears against the contact and whether the follow-up workflow still shows a scheduled message. Repeat with a staff-created booking if that is a route your team uses.

Use a confirmed booking as the signal to end the sales chase. Scope the change to the relevant calendar or campaign if one contact can book several services. Preserve the appointment confirmation and reminders. Use [HighLevel’s workflow resources](https://help.gohighlevel.com/) to check the options available in your account.

## Test replies and cancellations separately

A reply may need a person to review it even without a booking. Decide who owns that response and whether reminders pause. Decide what a cancellation should trigger, such as an invitation to reschedule. Test a cancelled appointment and a new one as separate cases so a delayed message from the first booking does not change the second. For moved appointments, see our [rescheduling guide](/blog/gohighlevel-rescheduled-appointment-reminders).
