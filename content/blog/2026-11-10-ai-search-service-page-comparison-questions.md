When someone asks whether they need an Elementor site or a custom theme, they're often deciding who will maintain it and how much flexibility they need. A service page that says “built for growth” leaves them with the same decision.

## Use the questions people ask before a quote

Take three anonymised questions from sales calls or enquiries. Look for a genuine trade-off. A useful answer might say that an editor-friendly build works well for a small marketing team publishing weekly, while a custom theme can make sense when the layouts and integrations need tighter control. Name the assumptions that would change your answer.

Put the explanation next to the service detail it qualifies. Show one relevant project or an annotated sample if you have permission to share it. Explain what was built and what you can verify. A concrete example of a migration or editing workflow is useful even without a dramatic result metric.

## Make it easy to find and verify

Google's [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) describes the role of standard Search eligibility and SEO practices. Check that your page can be crawled, that the comparison exists as visible text, and that related pages link to it with descriptive anchors. For instance, our [Elementor versus custom theme guide](/blog/elementor-vs-custom-wordpress-theme) gives readers a place to explore that decision in more detail.

Ask someone outside the project to explain, in their own words, when they should choose each option. Where they hesitate, replace a vague claim with an example, condition or limit. Track whether the page starts attracting more relevant enquiries. Inclusion in a specific AI answer is a separate outcome.
