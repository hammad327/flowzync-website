SEO for New Websites: What Actually Matters in the First 90 Days

June 17, 20262 min readUpdated October 27, 2026
Published by FlowzyncEditorial policy & correctionsReport an error

In this guide

  1. Days 1–7: make sure Google can read you
  2. Days 7–30: one page, one purpose
  3. Days 30–60: earn your structure
  4. Days 60–90: consistency beats intensity
  5. What to ignore for now
  6. Sources and scope

New websites don't need advanced SEO. They need the fundamentals done properly — because most sites never get even those right.

Days 1–7: make sure Google can read you

  • Submit your sitemap in Google Search Console
  • Check every important page is indexable (no stray "noindex" tags)
  • Fix any pages that return errors
  • Make sure the mobile experience works — Google indexes mobile-first

None of this is glamorous. All of it is mandatory.

Days 7–30: one page, one purpose

Give distinct services their own pages when they answer different customer needs. Use a clear title, descriptive heading, scope, examples and a relevant next step. There is no minimum word count for Google indexing or AdSense approval; a complete concise answer is better than padding a page to 500 words.

Use specific titles that describe the page, such as "Custom Website Design Services", rather than a vague label. A title alone cannot guarantee better rankings.

Days 30–60: earn your structure

  • Answer real buying questions where useful. FAQ content helps readers; Google no longer displays its FAQ rich-result feature, so do not promise dropdowns.
  • Interlink related pages naturally — service to service, blog to service
  • Publish your first two or three articles answering real questions your customers ask

Days 60–90: consistency beats intensity

Publish when you have something useful and distinct to add. A fixed weekly schedule is not a ranking requirement. Improving an existing guide can be more useful than publishing another article covering the same question.

What to ignore for now

Domain authority scores, keyword density percentages, and anyone promising "#1 rankings guaranteed." Foundations first; the rest is noise until you have traffic to optimise.

SEO for a new site isn't a trick. It's making it effortless for Google to understand who you help and how.

Our WordPress builds and custom websites ship with all of the above configured — but if you have an existing site, these steps are exactly where we'd start an audit.

Frequently asked

What SEO should a new website have from day one?

A unique title and description on every page, one clear H1, a correct canonical URL, a sitemap submitted to Search Console, fast mobile pages, and a page for each thing you want to be found for. None of that is optional and all of it is cheaper to do at build time than to retrofit.

How long does SEO take to work on a new site?

Timing varies with discovery, competition, site quality and the query. Some pages are processed quickly and others may remain unindexed. Submit a sitemap, inspect important URLs and measure actual results rather than promising a fixed timeline.

Do I need to blog for SEO?

Only if your customers research before buying and you will keep it going. For many local service businesses, a complete Google Business Profile and steady reviews produce more enquiries than a blog ever would.
