Write a Website Case Study Without Inventing Metrics

November 20, 20262 min read
Published by FlowzyncEditorial policy & correctionsReport an error

You have screenshots and a useful project story, but the client never tracked conversion rates. There is still enough to write a case study if you focus on what you can show.

Explain what changed and why

Start with the brief. A service business might have needed to separate emergency requests from planned work. Show the two contact routes and explain what the team wanted to know about each enquiry. A screenshot and a short caption can demonstrate a design decision without claiming how it performed.

Choose evidence you can share

Use approved screenshots, a short walkthrough or a before-and-after flow. Give enough context for someone outside your industry to understand the change. If you share client feedback, get permission and quote it accurately. Mark sample projects as concepts and explain the constraints you assumed.

Link the case study from a related service page. Give clients a one-page version they can share in a supplier or project announcement if it serves their audience. Our home services portfolio example illustrates the distinction between urgent calls and planned enquiries.

Frequently asked

What if the client will not allow screenshots?

Ask whether an anonymised process diagram or written account is acceptable. Honour any restrictions they give you.

Should I add estimated conversion improvements?

Use measured results only when you can explain their source and period. Demonstrate the change with the evidence you have.
